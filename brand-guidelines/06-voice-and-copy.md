# 06 Voice and copy

Grounded, plainspoken, specific. No em dashes. Ever.

## How we sound

Confident, plainspoken, grounded. Short declarative sentences. Talk to "you" and "your business." The product does things on your behalf. No hype, no hedging, no exclamation points.

## Lead with payments

Most readers come to EPD Commerce to accept payments. Open with what they get, then explain the tools. See the messaging hierarchy in `01-brand-at-a-glance.md`.

## Contrast framing

Use sparingly and only where it fits the audience.

- Most platforms tell you a payment failed. EPD Commerce does something about it. (any audience)
- Most AI helps you write faster. EPD Commerce helps you run better. (AI-focused audiences only)

## Do

- Use sentence case in headlines.
- Name the feature, then say what it does: "Revenue Recovery retries failed subscription payments."
- Ground claims in specifics, using only approved claims from `data/claims.json`.
- Keep the soft words in timing and fit claims: "often same day," "typically 1 to 3 days," "generally under $25K."
- Use commas, periods, or parentheses for pauses.
- Write numbers as numerals: 12+, $25K, 1 to 3 days.

## Don't

- Use Title Case Headlines That Feel Corporate.
- Open with abstract nouns: "A solution for your catalog."
- Lowercase feature names in body copy, or replace them with "our recovery system" or "our AI."
- Use hype words: revolutionary, game-changing, supercharge.
- Use em dashes.
- Say approval is guaranteed, or promise placement in AI assistants.
- Reuse numbers from demos, dashboards, or screenshots as claims.
- Name banking partners. Say "one of our banking partners."
- Say "AI runs your business." Say what a named feature does.

## Naming

All names, approved terms, and never-use lists are in `data/names.json`. That file wins over any other document in this repo.
